# MUSEUM HOURS

![](/images/211.jpg)

> UNBOWED IN THE MUSEUM CORNER

UNBOWED prefers
the dark museum corner.

Not the center hall.
Not the rotunda.
Not the marble staircase
where school groups learn
how reverence echoes.

No.

It waits beside
the emergency exit,
half-hidden by a plaque
about sacrifice,
which is brave of the plaque,
considering.

Here, everything has labels.

Flags.
Uniforms.
Letters home.
A watch stopped at impact.
A helmet with a dent
important enough
to deserve lighting.

UNBOWED studies the glass cases.

So this is how it works.

Break clearly enough
and they give you velvet.

Die neatly enough
and they give you a caption.

Become evidence
and they lower the room temperature
for your preservation.

But remain standing?

Continue?

Carry the weight
without producing
a photogenic ruin?

Then congratulations.

You are furniture.

So UNBOWED lurks
where the docent’s voice
does not reach.

Dark limestone.
No spotlight.
No gift shop miniature.
No tasteful magnet
that says
I endured and all I got was
this continued expectation.

It does not haunt the museum
because it hates memory.

It haunts the museum
because memory has favorites.

Because recognition
moves through these rooms
with white gloves
and selective vision.

Because somewhere nearby,
under a perfect cone of light,
a broken thing is being honored
for failing beautifully.

UNBOWED remains upright
in the corner.

Unlit.

Uncaptioned.

Not collapsed enough
to be precious.

Not dead enough
to be sacred.

Still waiting
for someone to notice
that standing there
has been the whole injury.
