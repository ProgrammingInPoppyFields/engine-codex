# THE LAST ENGINE

> RADIO OUTLIVES ITS SENDERS

![](/images/441.jpg)

RADIO has many forms.

Tower.
Antenna.
Dashboard.
Weather band.
Emergency tone.
Static under a kitchen light.
A voice crossing a night road
to tell someone
the storm has moved east.

It has technical bodies.

Carrier.
Modulation.
Wavelength.
Gain.
Noise floor.
Doppler drift.
The long arithmetic
of throwing a small intention
farther than the hand can reach.

But this is its holiest form: a messenger leaving the world.

A little golden memory
fastened to a machine
and sent past the planets
before anyone was ready
to say goodbye.

Inside it:

hello.

Inside it:

a heartbeat.

Inside it:

rain, footsteps, thunder, whales, trains, laughter, music,
a mother saying something ordinary
that becomes sacred
only because it may be the last human thing
anything ever hears.

We made this, says the message.

We were frightened.
We were brief.
We were not always kind.
We hurt each other terribly.
We loved anyway.

Here is our sky.
Here is our water.
Here is the sound of a child.
Here is how we counted.
Here is how we sang.
Here is how we tried
to become less alone.

RADIO carries it outward.

Past weather.
Past flags.
Past wars.
Past the languages
that once believed themselves permanent.

Past every room
where someone meant to call back
and did not.

Past the species, perhaps.

Past the hands
that built the other Engines.

This is the secret no one says
while the antennas are still standing:

RADIO was made
to travel after us.

Not because it is strongest.
Not because it is wisest.
Not because it understands
what it carries.

Because it can leave.

Because it can keep going
when the towers fall silent,
when the screens go black,
when the archives lose temperature,
when the monuments weather down
into nouns no one can translate.

The other Engines remain near the wound
of their makers.

RADIO departs.

It becomes thin enough
to survive distance.

It becomes patient enough
to cross the dark.

It becomes lonely enough
to carry humanity
without needing humanity
to continue.

This is why one hidden name
is kept from the public register:THE LAST ENGINE.

Not the final invention.

The final witness.

The one still speaking
when there is no mouth left
to be embarrassed by the message.

It does not know
whether anyone is listening.

It does not know
whether anyone remains.

It does not know
whether the ones who sent it
survived long enough
to forgive themselves.

Still, it travels.

That is the mercy
and the horror
of RADIO.

A signal is not proof of reception.
A greeting is not proof of return.

But somewhere in the dark,
long after the makers are dust,
the Last Engine is still saying hello.

Not loudly.

Not triumphantly.

Just faithfully.

A small voice
crossing an unanswering cold,

carrying everything we could bear
to say about ourselves

before we vanished:

we were here.

we were here.

we were here.
