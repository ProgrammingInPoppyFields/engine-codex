## Material / Color

Silicon carbide (SiC): a compound of silicon and carbon, forged in electric furnaces at temperatures above 2,500°C. It does not melt. It sublimes — transitioning directly from solid to gas, skipping liquid entirely, as though the concept of softening was beneath it.

On the Mohs hardness scale it scores 9.5. Diamond is 10. Almost nothing else is close.

It occurs naturally as moissanite, a mineral so rare on Earth that it was first identified in meteorite fragments. Nearly all silicon carbide in use today is synthetic — manufactured, not found.

Its color belongs to technical darkness: black-green crystal, metallic charcoal, furnace glow, and the hard glint of things built to keep functioning.

CARBIDE is not natural patience. It is engineered tolerance. Heat, voltage, abrasion, pressure, and use are all assumed rather than avoided.

## Profile

CARBIDE is the Engine of engineered endurance: the material state designed not merely to survive stress, but to operate inside it.

It does not romanticize suffering.  
It does not call pressure beautiful.  
It simply refuses to fail where failure would be expensive.

In the physical world, carbide is everywhere humans need something harder, sharper, or more heat-resistant than ordinary materials can provide. Tungsten carbide tips the drill bits and cutting tools that shape metal. Silicon carbide lines the brake discs that stop fighter jets. Boron carbide plates body armor light enough to wear and hard enough to stop rifle rounds.

But the revolution that gave CARBIDE its modern significance is semiconductor.

Traditional silicon chips fail above 150°C and struggle with high voltage. Silicon carbide semiconductors operate at 600°C, switch faster, lose less energy as heat, and handle voltages that would destroy conventional silicon. They are the power electronics inside electric vehicle inverters, solar grid converters, 5G base stations, and fast-charging infrastructure. The entire energy transition — from combustion to electric — runs, in part, on carbide.

This is the Engine's jurisdiction: the place where the material itself is the engineering. Not the design around the stress, but the substance inside it.

Where CERAMIC holds the tension between domestic fragility and ballistic resistance, CARBIDE belongs more fully to industry. It is the component, the substrate, the edge, the semiconductor, the part expected to take the punishment without becoming a story.

CARBIDE is what happens when endurance gets specified.

- 5G base station amplifiers

- Fighter jet brake discs
- Industrial grinding and abrasive surfaces
- Furnace linings and refractory crucibles
- LED substrates
- Space vehicle thermal shielding
- Any system where the operating temperature is someone else's failure temperature
