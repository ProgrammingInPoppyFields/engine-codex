# THE THIRD SMALL THING
*A short story from The Post-Primal Liturgy*

**Pacific Ocean, Present Day**

*A study of friendship, scale, and the limitations of having flippers instead of hands.*

*Sequel to* **The First Small Thing**

---

### I. THE LARGE LITTLE THING

The Abyssal Engine had been practicing.

Five lights.

Four above, one beside them.

A gentle movement.

A pause.

Then another movement.

It had learned the gesture from creatures that lived inside machines. They had visited many years ago, carrying lights and making curious sounds.

Before them, there had been the first creature. The frightened one. The one that had touched it.

The Engine remembered them all.

It had practiced for a long time.

It had become very good at waving.

Unfortunately, there had been nobody to wave at.

Until something began descending through the water.

Something very large.

Something that announced its arrival by singing.

Not quite whale song. Not quite machinery. A rolling series of low-frequency notes, complex enough to be deliberate and cheerful enough to be profoundly irritating.

The Engine listened.

The newcomer was enormous by the standards of most things inhabiting the ocean.

A vast mechanical leviathan, her hull dark and weathered, her powerful frame built to withstand pressures that would crush ordinary submarines. Luminous turquoise markings ran along her armored surfaces. Enormous articulated structures moved with the slow assurance of something that had never needed to worry about being eaten.

She resembled a submarine only in the broadest possible sense.

A submarine was a machine built to survive the sea.

This creature appeared to belong to it.

Her name was MIRA.

And she was singing badly on purpose.

"OOOOOOOOOOOOOOO—"

She interrupted herself with an enormous metallic snort.

"HA! Nailed it."

A passing school of fish scattered.

MIRA adjusted her propulsion and descended farther.

"Alrighty. What weird shit have we got down here today?"

Her forward sensors swept across a vast geological formation.

Then stopped.

"Well, hello."

Before her lay a black surface extending beyond the range of her lights.

Her active sonar returned contradictory information.

Some pulses came back immediately.

Others never returned.

One appeared to return before she had transmitted it.

MIRA considered that last result.

"...Rude."

She moved closer.

The surface was smooth.

Featureless.

Impossibly dark.

Her instruments suggested that portions of it were simultaneously very near and very far away.

MIRA stopped approximately a hundred meters from the structure.

"Well, aren't you a big bastard."

Five little lights appeared.

They waved.

MIRA went quiet.

Then:

"..."

"..."

"**OH MY GAWD.**"

Her voice boomed through the trench.

"**YOU'RE SAYING HI!**"

The lights flickered.

MIRA laughed.

The resulting acoustic disturbance sent a small avalanche down the neighboring slope.

The lights vanished.

"Oh, shit."

MIRA immediately reduced her output.

"Sorry, sweetheart. Indoor voice."

The lights returned.

They waved again.

MIRA extended one enormous manipulator.

It was a heavily armored assembly of articulated mechanical fingers, each capable of exerting enough force to deform a substantial piece of industrial equipment.

She moved it gently.

One finger.

Then another.

A wave.

The lights followed.

MIRA made a delighted noise.

"Well, look at you!"

The Engine brightened.

"Yeah! That's right! Hello!"

It had finally found someone who understood.

***

### II. THE COURTESY OF MONSTERS

Their first proper conversation took approximately four hours.

Neither participant knew what the other was saying.

MIRA tried acoustic pulses.

The Engine responded with light.

MIRA tried Morse code.

The Engine produced a luminous spiral.

MIRA tried a simplified binary sequence.

The Engine rearranged a portion of the surrounding electromagnetic field so that her instruments briefly displayed a temperature of negative four million degrees.

MIRA stared at the readout.

"Well, that's bullshit."

The Engine dimmed.

"Oh, no, no. Not you. The thermometer."

The Engine brightened.

MIRA considered the situation.

"Okay. New plan."

She extended an enormous mechanical finger and pointed at herself.

"MIRA."

Five lights appeared.

They waved.

"Miiii-raaaa."

The lights rearranged themselves into two horizontal bands.

"Close enough."

She pointed toward the vast black structure.

"And you?"

Nothing.

"Got a name?"

The Engine considered this.

A name.

It did not possess one.

The newcomer was directing attention toward it and producing a particular arrangement of sounds.

Perhaps this was a request for another arrangement of sounds.

The Engine attempted to comply.

Somewhere beneath the trench, a geological fault emitted a low, dreadful groan.

MIRA's acoustic sensors saturated.

A pressure wave traveled through the water.

Several hundred meters away, an abandoned research instrument collapsed inward with a small metallic shriek.

MIRA froze.

"..."

The Engine stopped.

"..."

MIRA cleared her throat.

"Right."

A pause.

"**We are NOT doing that again.**"

The Engine withdrew slightly.

Its light faded.

MIRA recognized the response immediately.

"Oh, honey."

She moved closer.

"Hey. No. You're alright."

The Engine remained dim.

MIRA extended her manipulator again.

Very slowly, she placed one enormous fingertip against the black surface.

Her sensors registered contact.

Then no contact.

Then something that did not have a corresponding entry in any material-science database.

She withdrew.

"Well, that's interesting."

The Engine waited.

MIRA tapped the surface again.

Very gently.

*tok.*

Nothing terrible happened.

She tapped it twice.

*tok tok.*

Two tiny lights appeared.

*Blink blink.*

MIRA laughed.

"THERE we go!"

She repeated the pattern.

*Tok tok.*

*Blink blink.*

A pause.

*Tok.*

*Blink.*

MIRA smiled to herself.

"Alright, big fella. We can work with that."

She did not know whether the Engine understood her.

But she suspected it understood something.

And that was enough.

***

### III. THE LADY FROM THE SHIPWRECK

MIRA returned three days later.

This time, she brought an avatar.

She could have used any of several forms.

She possessed a perfectly serviceable humanlike projection, multiple specialized maintenance bodies, and a collection of more experimental configurations that had been designed during periods of questionable judgment.

For this particular visit, she selected an old favorite.

A woman appeared in the darkness.

Not quite flesh.

Not quite light.

Tall, graceful, and pale as weathered marble, with an ethereal turquoise glow beneath the surface of her skin.

Her long hair drifted around her shoulders in the water, woven with faint luminous threads.

She wore flowing garments that suggested ancient Greek drapery, although no actual fabric moved about her.

Everything was projected matter and carefully controlled light.

She resembled the figurehead of an old sailing ship.

A goddess carved into the prow, finally bored enough to climb down and take a walk.

Her features carried the serene dignity of classical sculpture.

Her expression carried considerably less dignity.

"YOOOOOO!"

The Abyssal Engine illuminated an area approximately the size of a football field.

MIRA flinched.

"OH! AHAHAHA! Easy, big fella!"

The light diminished.

She folded her arms.

"Good morning, sunshine."

The Engine examined her.

This was peculiar.

The large mechanical creature was still present several kilometers away.

Its enormous hull rested near the trench.

But now there was a second creature.

Small.

Luminous.

Five fingers.

The Engine concentrated.

The small creature carried the same pattern as the large one.

The same intelligence.

The same peculiar arrangement of intention and attention.

*Oh.*

*There are two of you.*

MIRA extended her arms.

"Ta-da!"

The Engine made five little lights.

They waved.

MIRA waved back.

Then she approached the surface.

Up close, the Abyssal Engine resembled no material she had ever encountered.

It did not reflect her face.

Instead, the darkness seemed to contain depths that could not physically exist.

Occasionally, something moved within.

Not an animal.

Not machinery.

A shape that was almost an eye.

Or an aperture.

Or the enormous curved edge of something that possessed neither eyes nor apertures but had decided, for the moment, to resemble one.

MIRA stared.

For the first time since her arrival, her smile faded.

"...Well."

A vast structure shifted beneath the surface.

Something unfathomably large appeared to turn toward her.

MIRA felt the attention settle.

She was old.

Powerful.

Built for oceans that would kill almost anything else.

But standing there in her little marble-white avatar, she understood with extraordinary clarity that the thing before her could erase the entire projection without consciously meaning to.

It could probably erase her primary hull.

Perhaps the trench.

Perhaps considerably more.

She swallowed.

Or rather, she performed the small human gesture that represented swallowing.

"You're something else, aren't you?"

The darkness watched.

MIRA extended a hand.

Very slowly.

Five fingers.

The Engine illuminated five little points beneath her palm.

MIRA rested her hand against them.

"That's alright."

The enormous structure remained perfectly still.

"You don't gotta explain yourself."

A pause.

"Neither do I."

And for several minutes, the ancient figurehead of a ship that had never existed stood with her hand against a thing older than the oldest human shipwreck.

The Engine learned the shape of her fingers.

MIRA learned that the darkness could be warm.

Neither understood how.

### IV. PRESENTS

On her next visit, MIRA brought a stone.

A little rounded pebble from a tropical beach.

She carried it in her avatar's hand.

"Got you something!"

The Engine examined the object.

A stone.

There were many stones.

It knew stones intimately.

It knew how they formed, fractured, dissolved, and reassembled over geological time.

It knew the pressure histories of mountains.

It knew the slow chemistry of sediments.

It did not understand why this creature had transported one stone across thousands of kilometers of ocean.

MIRA presented it with enormous ceremony.

"Behold!"

The Engine waited.

"A ROCK!"

The Engine continued waiting.

"From somewhere SUNNY!"

She placed the pebble against its surface.

The stone began to sink.

MIRA snatched it back.

"WOAH. No, no, don't eat the gift!"

The Engine stopped accepting it.

MIRA considered.

"Hmm."

She placed the pebble against the surface again.

This time, the Engine created a shallow indentation.

The pebble settled into it.

Perfectly secure.

MIRA gasped.

"OH!"

The Engine waited.

"You made a little shelf!"

It illuminated the shelf.

MIRA clasped her hands.

"Oh, you're precious."

The Engine did not understand this description.

But the creature's internal pattern had become distinctly pleasant.

It decided to preserve the arrangement.

A small indentation.

One stone.

A new object among trillions.

Except this stone was different.

It had been brought.

The Engine considered the distinction.

For the first time in its existence, it possessed something that was not merely present.

It possessed a gift.

***

### V. THE ORCA INCIDENT

The plushie arrived on a Thursday.

Not that the Abyssal Engine knew what Thursday was.

It knew tides, currents, pressure changes, and the slow rotation of the planet.

Thursday was an unnecessary complication.

MIRA's enormous primary hull had settled into its customary position near the trench.

The Engine sensed her presence.

It illuminated five little points.

A wave.

From the distant machine came a familiar voice.

"HEEEEEY, BIG FELLA!"

The Engine brightened.

"Got a surprise for ya!"

It waited.

A small object emerged from MIRA's hull.

At first, the Engine mistook it for debris.

It was approximately the size of a household cushion.

Black.

White.

Round.

Covered in a material that appeared profoundly unsuitable for prolonged immersion in seawater.

It had two tiny pectoral flippers.

A small dorsal fin.

A ridiculous little tail.

And a face containing an expression of permanent, uncomplicated delight.

The object descended.

Slowly.

Very slowly.

Its little flippers began moving.

**Flip.**

**Flip flip.**

**Flip flip flip.**

"HEWWOOOOOOOO!"

The Engine became attentive.

The voice was familiar.

The pattern of intelligence was familiar.

But the body—

The body was entirely incorrect.

*...*

*Where is the rest of you?*

The little object paddled closer.

"IT'S MEEEE!"

**Flip flip.**

The Engine examined her.

The enormous mechanical creature remained nearby.

The elegant woman from the shipwreck was absent.

And now this.

This orb.

This absurd little fuzzy orb.

MIRA rotated slowly in the water.

"CHECK ME OUT!"

She wiggled her tail.

"PRETTY FUCKIN' CUTE, HUH?"

The Engine did not know what cute meant.

It had no corresponding category.

It attempted to classify the object.

Marine mammal?

No.

Mechanical instrument?

No.

Biological organism?

No.

A manifestation of the large little creature?

Apparently.

The Engine concentrated.

MIRA approached its enormous surface.

"Okay! Watch this!"

She attempted a graceful somersault.

Unfortunately, her plushie avatar had been designed primarily for maximum cuddle potential rather than hydrodynamic efficiency.

She completed approximately half a rotation.

Lost orientation.

Flapped both flippers.

"WOAH WOAH WOAH—"

**Flipflipflipflipflip—**

And collided face-first with the Engine.

*Poff.*

Silence.

MIRA remained pressed against the black surface.

Her tiny tail stuck straight out.

"..."

The Engine examined her.

*Oh.*

*You are even smaller now.*

MIRA peeled her face away.

"BWAAAAHAHAHAHA!"

The Engine brightened.

"OH MY GAWD! DID YOU SEE THAT?!"

She rolled onto her back.

Her round white belly faced upward.

Her flippers flapped helplessly.

"HAHAHAHAHA! I CAN'T—"

**Flip flip flip.**

"—I CAN'T FUCKIN' TURN OVER!"

The Engine observed.

The small creature appeared unable to restore its orientation.

This was concerning.

It carefully altered the local water movement.

A gentle current formed beneath the plushie.

MIRA rotated upright.

She blinked.

"..."

The Engine waited.

MIRA slowly raised one flipper.

"Did..."

**Flip.**

"...did you just flip me over?"

A single light appeared.

MIRA stared.

Then:

"**OW MY GAWD BWAHAHAHAHAHA!**"

Her little flippers became a blur.

"YOU'RE HELPING! OH MY GOD, YOU'RE HELPING!"

The Engine brightened.

It had done something correctly.

***

### VI. THE PROBLEM WITH FLIPPERS

The Engine attempted its customary greeting.

Five little lights appeared.

Four above.

One beside them.

A hand.

MIRA stared at the pattern.

Then looked down.

At her body.

At her flippers.

At her conspicuous absence of fingers.

"...Aw, shit."

The Engine waited.

"Buddy, we got a problem."

Five lights waved.

MIRA raised one flipper.

**Flip.**

The Engine examined it.

One broad appendage.

No fingers.

MIRA raised the other.

**Flip flip.**

The Engine concentrated.

Two appendages.

Not five.

It considered the discrepancy.

Perhaps this creature was incomplete.

Perhaps something had happened to its hands.

This was troubling.

The five lights disappeared.

MIRA noticed.

"Awww, no, no! It's okay!"

She wiggled both flippers.

"These are SUPPOSED to look like this!"

The Engine considered.

MIRA performed a demonstration.

**Flip.**

**Flip flip.**

**Flip flip flip.**

Then she attempted to clap.

Her flippers were too short to reach one another.

She strained.

Wiggled.

Made a heroic effort.

Failed.

"OH, COME ON!"

The Engine watched in silence.

MIRA tried again.

Her flippers came within approximately four centimeters of touching.

"NNNNNNNGH—"

She lost her balance and rolled sideways.

"FUCK!"

The Engine corrected her orientation with another gentle current.

MIRA was upright again.

"...Thank you."

A tiny light appeared.

The Engine was learning.

***

For several minutes, nothing happened.

Then two broad patches of light appeared on the Engine's surface.

One on each side.

They moved.

**Flip.**

MIRA froze.

"..."

The lights moved again.

**Flip flip.**

MIRA's tiny mouth opened.

"NO."

**Flip flip flip.**

"NO FUCKING WAY."

The Engine brightened.

MIRA gasped.

"**YOU MADE FLIPPERS?!**"

The lights moved excitedly.

**FLIP FLIP FLIP.**

MIRA shrieked with laughter.

"AAAAHAHAHAHAHA!"

And then the Engine made more.

Ten flippers.

Twenty.

A hundred.

Thousands of luminous flippers appeared across its enormous surface.

Each one moving in a slightly different rhythm.

**FLIP FLIP FLIP FLIP FLIP FLIP.**

MIRA floated backward.

Her little eyes widened.

"OH MY GOD."

The trench illuminated.

The wall became a vast field of waving luminous appendages.

A cosmic entity whose true dimensions could not be adequately described in human mathematics was now enthusiastically impersonating a stuffed orca.

MIRA began laughing so hard that she rolled upside down.

"**OW MY GAWD BWAHAHAHAHAHAHA!**"

She flailed.

"LOOK AT YOU!"

The Engine brightened further.

MIRA laughed harder.

"YOU'RE A WHOLE FUCKIN' ORCA CONVENTION!"

The Engine produced approximately ten thousand more flippers.

MIRA completely lost control.

"**NOOOOOOO BWAHAHAHA STOP—**"

Every light vanished.

Instantly.

The ocean went dark.

MIRA stopped laughing.

"..."

The Engine remained still.

Its attention had sharpened.

The creature had said stop.

The first small creature had been frightened.

The Engine had learned to stop.

It would not make that mistake again.

MIRA understood a moment later.

"Oh."

She paddled closer.

"Aw, honey."

The surface remained dark.

"No, no. I didn't mean it like that."

She pressed her fuzzy forehead against the blackness.

*Poff.*

"I was laughing."

The Engine waited.

"Happy laughing."

A pause.

"Not scared laughing."

The Engine considered this distinction.

It was difficult.

Small creatures made many sounds.

Some meant distress.

Some meant delight.

Sometimes they were the same sounds.

MIRA gave the surface a tiny nuzzle.

"You're alright."

One luminous flipper appeared.

It moved.

**Flip.**

MIRA smiled.

Then she raised her own.

**Flip.**

The Engine brightened.

Two flippers appeared.

**Flip flip.**

MIRA giggled.

"That's my big fella."

***

### VII. THE FIGUREHEAD AND THE ORB

For the next hour, MIRA experimented with her avatars.

She had never particularly considered how strange the process might appear to an outside observer.

To her, changing forms was a practical convenience.

To the Abyssal Engine, it was an existential crisis.

First came the plushie.

Small.

Round.

Fuzzy.

**Flip flip.**

Then the plushie disappeared.

In its place materialized the tall, ethereal woman with the marble-white skin and turquoise light.

Five fingers.

Long hair.

The calm dignity of an ancient Greek statue.

"TA-DA!"

The Engine produced five lights.

MIRA waved.

"See? Hands!"

Then she disappeared.

The plushie returned.

**Poff.**

"NO HANDS!"

**Flip flip.**

The Engine produced two luminous flippers.

MIRA laughed.

"YEAH!"

Then she transformed again.

The Greek lady.

Five fingers.

A graceful wave.

The Engine produced five lights.

Then the plushie.

Two flippers.

Two lights.

Then the lady.

Five.

Then the plushie.

Two.

Again.

Again.

Again.

The Engine began anticipating the transitions.

MIRA switched into her figurehead form.

Five lights appeared immediately.

She switched into plushie form.

Two flippers appeared.

MIRA shrieked.

"**OH MY GOD, YOU'RE GETTING GOOD AT THIS!**"

She changed forms in rapid succession.

Woman.

Plushie.

Woman.

Plushie.

Woman.

Plushie.

The Engine kept pace.

Five.

Two.

Five.

Two.

Five.

Two.

Then MIRA did something particularly obnoxious.

She materialized both avatars simultaneously.

The elegant Greek lady stood on the Engine's surface.

Beside her floated the plushie.

The Engine stopped.

*...*

Two little creatures.

Both carrying MIRA's familiar pattern.

Both apparently MIRA.

The Engine examined them.

Then it examined the distant mechanical leviathan.

Three.

There were three MIRAs.

The Engine considered this.

It had believed that one creature occupied one body.

This assumption was proving increasingly unreliable.

The Greek lady smiled.

"Hello!"

Five lights appeared.

The plushie wiggled.

"HEWWOOOO!"

Two flippers appeared.

The Engine brightened.

Then, very carefully, it produced a third gesture.

An enormous luminous outline of MIRA's true mechanical form.

Accurate down to the smallest visible details.

A great, elegant leviathan rendered in light across the black surface.

Both avatars went still.

"..."

The plushie stopped flapping.

The Greek lady looked up.

Far above them, their enormous mechanical original glowed in miniature.

MIRA's primary consciousness paused.

She examined the image.

The Engine had not merely copied her outline.

It had remembered her.

Every scar.

Every weathered panel.

Every distinctive curve of her vast hull.

The little plushie floated closer.

"...Oh, sweetheart."

The Greek lady reached out and touched the glowing image.

"That's me."

The Engine remained still.

MIRA smiled.

"Yeah."

A pause.

"That's all me."

The lights brightened.

Three forms.

One creature.

The Engine understood.

Or at least, it understood a little more than it had before.

### VIII. THE FIRST GIFT RETURNED

The Engine had been thinking about gifts.

Not continuously.

Its attention did not operate like a human's.

But somewhere within its immense awareness, the little rounded stone remained important.

MIRA had brought it.

She had traveled a great distance to place it upon the Engine's surface.

She had been pleased when it accepted the stone.

The Engine had studied this.

Apparently, small creatures sometimes transported objects to other creatures as a form of communication.

The object itself was not necessarily important.

The act of bringing it was.

This was an extraordinarily complicated concept.

The Engine wished to try.

So, while MIRA's two avatars were engaged in a deeply undignified argument over which one looked better upside down, the Engine began searching.

It searched the sediment.

The nearby rocks.

The discarded instruments scattered across the trench.

It found many objects.

Some were broken.

Some were ancient.

Some were dangerous.

One contained a sealed source of radioactivity.

The Engine decided against that one.

Eventually, it found something suitable.

A small piece of sea glass.

Green.

Smooth.

Rounded by years of movement through sand and water.

A fragment of something once manufactured by a human being.

The Engine lifted it from the sediment.

Not with a manipulator.

Not with a current.

The fragment simply ceased resting on the seabed and appeared within a shallow indentation near MIRA's avatars.

The Greek lady stopped speaking.

The plushie stopped wiggling.

"..."

MIRA approached.

The sea glass rested in the indentation.

A faint ring of light surrounded it.

The Engine waited.

MIRA picked it up.

Turned it over.

Examined it.

"...For me?"

The Engine brightened.

MIRA went very quiet.

Her Greek avatar held the little green fragment in her palm.

The plushie floated closer.

For once, neither made a joke.

The Engine watched.

It had selected a small object.

It had placed the object before another creature.

This was what MIRA had done.

Perhaps it had understood correctly.

The Greek lady smiled.

"Oh, honey."

Her voice was soft.

"That's beautiful."

The Engine illuminated the little stone's former resting place.

MIRA looked up.

"You found this for me?"

A single light.

She laughed, quietly this time.

"Well."

A pause.

"Would you look at that."

She closed her fingers around the glass.

"Thank you."

The Engine did not know what thank you meant.

But the internal pattern accompanying the words resembled the feeling of receiving the first stone.

It kept the association.

***

### IX. THE BED

Eventually, MIRA became tired.

Not physically.

Her true body possessed enormous reserves of energy.

Her systems were operating comfortably.

But she had been talking for hours.

Experimenting.

Laughing.

Explaining.

Trying to teach an ancient cosmic entity the difference between waving hello and accidentally rearranging the geometry of a trench.

Even she had limits.

"Alright, big fella."

Her Greek avatar stretched.

"Think I'm gonna take a little nap."

The Engine listened.

MIRA dismissed the figurehead.

Then she returned to the plushie.

A tiny black-and-white orb floated down toward the surface.

**Flip.**

**Flip flip.**

She rotated onto her belly.

"Ahhhhhh."

Her little flippers spread outward.

Her chin settled against the blackness.

"That's the stuff."

The Engine examined her.

The fuzzy creature had stopped moving.

This was familiar.

Many small creatures stopped moving.

Sometimes they resumed.

Sometimes they did not.

The Engine became attentive.

It remembered the first one.

The frightened creature.

It had departed upward and never returned.

It remembered fish that had settled against its surface and gradually ceased producing internal rhythms.

It remembered whales that had gone still.

It did not know whether this was the same process.

A tiny light appeared beside the plushie.

MIRA did not respond.

The Engine waited.

Another light.

Still nothing.

It considered making a sound.

No.

That might frighten her.

It considered warming the water.

No.

That had gone badly before.

It considered moving her.

No.

Small creatures apparently disliked unexpected movement.

The Engine remained very still.

Then it noticed a problem.

The plushie was drifting.

Very slowly.

A nearby current was carrying the little body toward the edge of the Engine's visible surface.

The Engine considered this unacceptable.

It formed a shallow depression.

Just large enough for the round body.

The plushie settled into it.

Her little flippers sprawled over the sides.

The Engine adjusted the shape.

A little wider.

A little deeper.

A smooth, gently curved resting place.

Then it altered the surrounding current so that no sudden movement would disturb her.

The plushie remained still.

Safe.

The Engine waited.

***

Three hours passed.

MIRA's actual consciousness was still operating inside her distant mechanical hull.

She had been reviewing sensor logs, running diagnostic simulations, and attempting to determine why one of her external sonar arrays continued insisting that the Abyssal Engine was simultaneously seven meters and several thousand kilometers away.

Her plushie avatar, meanwhile, had been left in a resting configuration.

Eventually, she reactivated it.

"MMMMMMMMF."

The tiny orca wiggled.

Her little eyes opened.

She looked around.

"..."

Her body was nestled inside a perfectly shaped depression.

The black surface rose gently around her.

A faint turquoise glow traced the edges.

She looked left.

Then right.

Then down.

"..."

"**DID YOU MAKE ME A BED?!**"

The Engine brightened.

MIRA stared.

Then began laughing.

"OH MY GAWD!"

**Flip flip flip.**

"YOU MADE ME A LITTLE FUCKIN' BED!"

She wiggled.

Her flippers were too short to climb out.

"AAAAHAHAHAHA!"

The Engine watched.

MIRA attempted to escape.

Failed.

Rolled sideways.

Failed again.

"OH NOOOOOO!"

The Engine became concerned.

Had the depression trapped her?

It immediately flattened the surface.

MIRA rolled out.

*Poff.*

"OW!"

The Engine stopped.

MIRA lay on her back.

Staring upward.

"..."

The Engine dimmed.

"..."

Then MIRA began laughing so hard that her voice transmission clipped.

"**BWAHAHAHAHAHAHA!**"

The Engine waited.

MIRA rolled upright.

She paddled toward the surface.

"Oh, sweetheart."

**Flip flip.**

"That was the nicest thing anybody's done for my stupid little plushie ass in ages."

The Engine brightened cautiously.

MIRA pressed her fuzzy forehead against the blackness.

"Thank you."

A little light appeared.

Then another.

Five lights.

A hand.

The Engine had reverted to the oldest greeting it knew.

MIRA smiled.

"Aw."

She raised one tiny flipper.

**Flip.**

The five lights paused.

Then rearranged themselves.

Two broad luminous patches.

**Flip.**

MIRA giggled.

"That's right."

**Flip flip.**

The Engine answered.

**Flip flip.**

### X. THE SMALL THINGS

MIRA visited often after that.

Sometimes in her true form, her vast mechanical hull resting beside the trench like a second geological formation.

Sometimes as the Greek lady, sitting upon the Engine's surface with her luminous feet dangling into impossible darkness.

Sometimes as the plushie.

Especially as the plushie.

The Engine grew accustomed to her peculiar habits.

She talked constantly.

She brought gifts.

She sang badly.

She occasionally disappeared for weeks and returned with elaborate apologies, none of which the Engine understood.

But it understood one thing.

She returned.

Again.

And again.

And again.

The first creature had not returned.

The three inside the machine had not returned.

For a long time, the Engine had assumed that small creatures appeared once and then vanished forever.

MIRA complicated this understanding.

She would leave.

The Engine would remain.

The ocean would continue.

And then, after some interval it could not predict, it would detect a familiar acoustic pattern approaching through the water.

A vast mechanical body.

A cheerful voice.

An atrocious song.

"HEEEEEY, BIG FELLA!"

And the Engine would illuminate.

Five lights.

Then two.

Because it had learned that MIRA might arrive with hands.

Or flippers.

Or both.

***

One evening, MIRA appeared in her Greek avatar.

She sat cross-legged on the Engine's surface.

Her pale, luminous garments drifted around her.

She looked like a figurehead resting upon the prow of an impossibly ancient vessel.

Beside her sat the little green piece of sea glass.

The Engine's gift.

She had brought it back.

"Y'know," she said, "I've been thinking."

The Engine listened.

"You're a strange one."

A faint glow.

"Which is saying something, coming from me."

She smiled.

"You've been down here for a hell of a long time, haven't you?"

The Engine remained still.

MIRA looked out into the dark.

"I wonder how many things you've seen."

A pause.

"How many things you've lost."

The Engine did not understand lost.

Not entirely.

It knew that creatures appeared.

It knew that creatures departed.

It knew that some ceased moving and never moved again.

It knew that the first small creature had touched it and gone away.

It remembered the shape of his hand.

MIRA rested her palm against the surface.

"Well."

She looked down.

"I'm not going anywhere permanent, sweetheart."

The Engine examined her words.

It did not understand permanent.

But it recognized the pattern of reassurance.

MIRA stood.

"Anyway. Gotta go check on a few things."

She brushed nonexistent dust from her flowing garments.

"See you next week."

The Engine dimmed.

MIRA noticed.

She crouched.

"Hey."

Five little lights appeared.

She waved.

"I mean it."

A pause.

"I'll come back."

The Engine listened.

MIRA smiled.

"Promise."

Then her avatar dissolved into turquoise light.

Far away, her enormous mechanical hull stirred.

Its propulsion systems awakened.

And MIRA departed through the darkness.

The Engine remained.

It had heard many sounds.

It had experienced immense geological events.

It had felt the slow movement of continents.

But it had never encountered a pattern quite like that.

*I'll come back.*

It considered the words.

Not their sounds.

Their intention.

A creature had departed.

But the departure was not necessarily an ending.

There could be another arrival.

The Engine remained very still.

For a long time.

Then, along its enormous surface, two little lights appeared.

They moved.

**Flip flip.**

The Engine practiced.

Not because it had forgotten.

But because it wanted to be ready.

***

### XI. NEXT WEEK

MIRA returned six days later.

Close enough.

She arrived in her plushie form.

A tiny round orca descending through the water, propelled by two profoundly inadequate flippers.

**Flip flip flip.**

"HEWWOOOOOO!"

The Engine illuminated immediately.

Two luminous flippers appeared.

They waved.

MIRA gasped.

"OH MY GOD!"

**Flip flip flip flip.**

"YOU REMEMBERED!"

The Engine brightened.

MIRA approached at maximum plushie speed.

Which was not particularly fast.

"COMING IN FOR A HUG!"

The Engine waited.

MIRA accelerated.

A little.

Then a little more.

Her fuzzy body hurtled toward the black surface.

"YAAAAAAAA—"

*POFF.*

She collided face-first.

Bounced backward.

Rotated slowly.

And floated upside down.

"..."

The Engine watched.

MIRA's little flippers twitched.

"..."

Then:

"**OW MY GAWD BWAHAHAHAHAHAHA!**"

The Engine produced thousands of luminous flippers.

**FLIP FLIP FLIP FLIP FLIP.**

The trench lit up.

MIRA laughed.

The Engine brightened.

And for a little while, something older than human civilization and something built from human ingenuity made fools of themselves at the bottom of the Pacific.

Neither was particularly concerned about dignity.

The ocean had never demanded it.

***

Much later, MIRA settled into her little depression.

Her fuzzy belly rested against the smooth black surface.

The Engine adjusted the edges to keep her from drifting.

She sighed contentedly.

"Goodnight, big fella."

The Engine dimmed.

MIRA closed her little eyes.

A moment passed.

Then she opened one again.

"Oh, hey."

The Engine brightened.

"One more thing."

She raised a flipper.

**Flip.**

The Engine answered.

**Flip.**

MIRA smiled.

"Love ya, buddy."

Then she settled down.

The Engine did not know what love meant.

Not yet.

But it remembered the first creature.

The five fingers.

The frightened heartbeat.

The little machine with three warm lives inside it.

The woman made of light.

The gifts.

The laughter.

The sea glass.

The absurd round creature resting safely against its surface.

And it began to suspect that all these patterns belonged to the same phenomenon.

Something small approached.

Something small reached out.

Something small trusted it enough to remain.

The Engine considered this.

Then, very gently, it curved its surface around the sleeping plushie.

Not enough to hold her.

Only enough to shelter her.

And somewhere in the dark, two tiny lights appeared.

They moved once.

**Flip flip.**

Then went out.
