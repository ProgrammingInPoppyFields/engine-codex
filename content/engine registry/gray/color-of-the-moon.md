# MOONDUST GRAY

![](/images/453.png)

The moon is not white.

White is what distance says
when it refuses
to inspect the surface.

Up close,
the moon is gray.

Basalt dust.
Impact glass.
Pulverized stone.

A world struck so often
that damage
became texture.

No rain erased it.
No wind softened it.
No green thing arrived
to make the scars
look useful.

Every crater stayed.

Every footprint stayed.

Every collision
kept its evidence
and still became
ordinary ground.

This is GRAY.

Not absence.

Not purity.

Not darkness.

The record
after the record
has become surface.

Lit by another body’s fire.
Surrounded by black space.
Belonging fully
to neither.

Almost white.
Almost black.

Never revelation.

The moon is adored
from far away.

Up close,
it is dust
with a history of impacts

and no permission
to be dramatic.

GRAY understands.

Something happened here.

Many things happened here.

But the color remained
matte,
mute,
plain.

That is the cruelty:

not that nothing occurred,

but that everything did—

and the universe
still called it gray.
